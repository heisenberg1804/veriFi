## Read this first

VeriFi is a forensic detector for AI-generated video. The engineering is largely complete; the system has never been validated on a dataset. The work of the last several sessions was **not** feature development — it was making validation cheap and finding out that prior measurements were wrong.

Three things a new session needs to know:

1. **The 10-video results in the old audit report are void.** They were measured through a frame sampler that returned wrong pixel data. Do not cite those numbers.
2. **Three of five detection signals return constants or near-constants.** Ensemble weight tuning is meaningless until they are fixed.
3. **No tuning decisions may be made on the 10-video test set.** It has structural confounds (see below) and n=10 with sub-0.01 margins is noise. This project has already made this mistake once, at n=5, in Phase 5.

## What was done, and why

### 1. Per-frame score persistence (the pivot)

**Problem:** the benchmark wrote per-signal *means* only. Re-tuning ensemble weights required a full re-inference pass, so testing 50 weight vectors meant 50 GPU runs.

**Why it mattered:** at that cost nobody runs a real search, so weights get hand-tuned on a single run — which is exactly the overfitting failure Phase 5 documented at n=5.

**Fix:** persist raw per-frame, per-signal scores to JSONL. Inference is paid once; every subsequent weight/threshold question is a CPU operation on a file.

**Design note:** the aggregator was refactored so scoring is a pure function, `score_from_signals(frame_signals, face_signals, weights)`, called by *both* the live pipeline and the offline tuner. Without this, the tuner optimizes a reimplementation that silently drifts from production and tuned weights don't reproduce.

### 2. Profiling before optimizing

Measured rather than guessed. Two findings:

- **CLIP was 83% of inference time**, at weight 0.15 and ranked 4th in reliability. Batching was implemented in the detector but the orchestrator called it one frame at a time, paying full MPS dispatch overhead per frame. Batching (chunked at 32) gave 2.1x. CLIP was deliberately *not* removed — the persistence work exists so data decides that, not intuition.
- **Frame selection cost ~17s regardless of video length** — 7,201 frames and 192 frames took the same time. This anomaly led to finding #3.

Net: 58s → 11s per video, bought before spending anything on a benchmark.

### 3. The seek bug (most important finding)

The frame selector used `cv2.VideoCapture.set(CAP_PROP_POS_FRAMES)` to seek to each candidate frame. **Past GOP boundaries, OpenCV returns wrong pixel data.** SocialSight diverged 100% after frame 200.

Consequences: histograms driving diversity selection were computed on wrong pixels, and the frames handed to the detectors were not the frames the selector believed it had chosen. Every measurement in the old audit report was taken through this.

**Fix:** two sequential passes — decode once retaining only per-frame stats (histogram, Laplacian, scene diff), run selection on those stats, decode again retaining pixels only for selected indices. Memory is bounded by the frame budget rather than video length; naively caching all frames would OOM on long video. Time dropped to under 1s.

### 4. Blur filter failure mode

Laplacian variance threshold was an absolute 100.0. The Perez interview scored max 54.4 across all 711 frames — soft-focus footage was **entirely unanalyzable**, the pipeline aborted with "No frames selected."

This would have been severe on any interview-heavy dataset. Fixed to fall back to the top-N sharpest available frames and flag `low_sharpness_fallback=True` rather than aborting. An absolute threshold across resolutions and bitrates remains questionable; a relative (percentile) gate is proposed but not implemented.

### 5. Label correction

Mansur/Neymar and SocialSight were labeled REAL in `calibrate_physics.py` and the CLIP heuristic labeler. Both are AI (Veo/Gemini, generated by the project owner). Fixed. The audit report had them correct; the scripts did not.

### Known bugs, not yet fixed

- **`smooth_score` is dead.** Holds 35% of DCT's weight, returns 0.000 for every video: the sigmoid centers at 1.0 while `gradient_variation` ranges 6.6–10.4, so it saturates. The raw `gradient_variation` feeding it has AUC 0.72 with correct polarity — the best-behaved component in the DCT stack, destroyed by a parameter mismatch.
- **`band_score` is clamped** to exactly 0.700 by a `low_ratio > 0.7` override on most videos.
- **ChCorr saturates** at 1.000 via `clip(avg_corr * 1.5, 0, 1)`.
- **EfficientNet is uncalibrated and may be actively harmful.** Scored 0.937 on the Perez interview — a real camera video — and was the main driver of a 0.663 false positive.

### Structural limitation in the tuner

`tune_weights.py` samples Dirichlet weights: non-negative, summing to 1. An anti-correlated signal can only be driven toward zero, never inverted. With DCT at AUC 0.12, the tuner's best available move is to discard the highest-weighted signal rather than use it correctly. **Signed weight search (polarity ∈ {+1, −1} per signal) is required before any benchmark tuning.**

## Why the 10-video test set cannot validate anything

| Confound | Detail |
|---|---|
| Face presence | 5/5 AI have faces; 2/5 real do. "Has a face" alone scores AUC 0.80. |
| Duration | AI mean 12s, real mean 115s. Scene counts 1–9 vs 1–142. Every temporal signal is confounded with clip length. |
| Compression | 100% H.264/mp4, all YouTube-re-encoded. Zero codec, container, or bitrate diversity. |
| Generators | 3 Veo, 2 unknown. No Sora, Runway, Kling, Pika, or any face-swap method. |

Additionally: the diagnostic script (`diagnose_signals.py`) uses 10 uniform frames and **does not run the face path**. Its ensemble numbers are not the pipeline's ensemble. Perez scores 0.421 in the diagnostic and 0.663 in the pipeline.

## Plan

### Now — dataset-independent
1. Fix `smooth_score` sigmoid parameters and remove the `band_score` override. These are parameter bugs; no amount of weight tuning recovers a constant.
2. Add signed weight search to `tune_weights.py`.
3. Add a local-folder benchmark adapter (manifest CSV: `path,label,generator,source`) so the harness can run on any directory, not just academic dataset layouts.

### Then — evaluation set
Build a modern-generator eval set with matched confounds (see "Dataset strategy").

### Then — the run
1. Pilot (n=20) to validate the harness. Purpose is plumbing, not results.
2. Calibration run with per-frame scores persisted. The one mandatory GPU spend, ~30 min at 11s/video.
3. Offline tuning: signed weights, threshold sweep, drop-one ablation. CPU only. Includes a frame-subsample study — if 16 frames match 60, all future runs get 4x cheaper.
4. Holdout run, everything frozen. **Report AUC from this only.**

### Open questions the tuner answers, not us
- Does CLIP earn its 83% compute cost?
- Is EfficientNet net-harmful?
- Is DCT's inversion real, and is `periodic_score` detecting H.264 macroblock structure in real footage rather than GAN checkerboarding in synthetic footage?

## Dataset strategy

The target domain is modern text-to-video (Veo, Sora, Kling, Runway). Celeb-DF v2 is 2020-era face-swap: fakes are synthesized *from* the real videos, so real and fake share identical backgrounds and differ only in the face region. Frame-level signals — noise residual and DCT, the ones actually working here — have little to measure. What remains discriminative is the double-encode artifact from the synthesis pipeline, which is a shortcut, not manipulation detection.

Celeb-DF therefore buys **external legibility** (comparable to published numbers) but tests a different system than the one built here.

An eval set for current generators is the more valuable asset and barely exists publicly. Design requirements:

- Uniform duration (8–10s) — removes the duration confound
- Balanced face presence across both classes — removes the face confound
- Content-matched pairs (AI sports clip ↔ real sports clip)
- **Identical re-encode for every video** (same ffmpeg settings) — otherwise the detector learns encoder provenance, not generation
- ≥3 generators represented
- Varied real sources, not all YouTube

Minimum viable: 40 AI + 40 real for calibration, same again held out.
